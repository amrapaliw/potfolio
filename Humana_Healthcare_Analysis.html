Analyst Commentary
Investment Thesis & Key Findings
01 — Revenue
Premium Engine Intact
Premium revenue grew from $74.2B (2020) to $122.8B (2025) — a 65% increase and 10.6% CAGR. Humana's Medicare Advantage membership has expanded steadily, and the company remains the #2 MA insurer in the US. Revenue growth is structurally intact; the problem is cost, not demand.
Premium CAGR: 10.6% · Services CAGR: 26.4%
02 — MLR Crisis
Medical Cost Spiral
MLR expanded from 83.1% (2020 — COVID lows) to 90.2% (2025). The core driver is a combination of post-COVID utilisation catch-up, Medicare Advantage risk-score recalibration by CMS, and behavioral health / post-acute care inflation. This is an industry-wide problem but hits Humana harder due to its MA concentration.
MLR expansion: +710bps over 5 years
03 — Earnings Collapse
Margin Compression
Net income fell from $3.4B (2020) to $1.2B (2025) — a 64% decline despite revenues nearly doubling. Operating margin compressed from 6.5% to 2.1%. EPS dropped from $25.31 to $9.84. The company faces a structural reset: it must reprice Medicare Advantage plans higher or exit unprofitable markets.
Net income ↓ 64% · EPS ↓ 61%
04 — Services Diversification
Care Delivery Growth
Services revenue grew at a 26.4% CAGR — from $1.8B to $5.8B — as Humana expanded into primary care clinics (CenterWell), pharmacy services, and home health. This diversification reduces the company's dependence on pure insurance premiums and builds vertically integrated care delivery capabilities.
Services mix: 1.4% (2020) → 4.5% (2025)
05 — Balance Sheet
Navigable Leverage
Long-term debt grew from $6.1B to $12.4B — doubled in 5 years. However, equity also grew from $13.7B to $17.7B. Debt/equity of 0.70x and interest coverage of 4.3x remain manageable. The company maintained its dividend throughout the earnings downturn, signalling management confidence in recovery.
D/E: 0.44x → 0.70x · Dividend held flat
06 — Key Risks
Watch Factors
1) CMS rate setting for Medicare Advantage — underfunding continues to compress MA economics. 2) Continued MLR overshoot if medical inflation persists. 3) Cash flow deterioration ($921M in 2025 vs $5.6B in 2020) limits financial flexibility. 4) Rising debt burden in a higher-rate environment increases interest expense. 5) Membership retention if plan exits occur.
Operating CFO ↓ 84% from 2020 peak
